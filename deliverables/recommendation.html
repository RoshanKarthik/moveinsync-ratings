The answer

The app is rated 4.64★, below Safetrax Commuter's 4.72★; 6.4% of its ratings are 1★ against 3.8% — and about 87% of those 1★ ratings come with a written review, while the 79% of raters who leave no text average 4.91★.

66% of the 769 1–2★ reviews of the last 365 days are about the app rather than the cab service; the costliest problem is “Crash, blank screen or won't open” (13% of 1–2★ reviews in the last 90 days, 19% in the last 30 days), followed by “Helpdesk unhelpful / complaints ignored”, “Login OTP not received”, “Hard to use / new UI worse” and “Frequent logout / re-login”. Meanwhile MoveInSync stopped answering reviews: 98% were answered in 2024Q1, 1% in the last 90 days, while Safetrax Commuter answers 100% of its 1–2★ reviews.

First, this week and with no release: answer every 1–2★ review, triage each new release with Android vitals, and put releases behind staged rollouts; then, in the next two releases, fix crashes, the false “not downloaded from Play Store” block and sign-in, show the boarding OTP before the seat-belt check, and start asking happy riders for a rating after good trips. If 1–2★ ratings fell to Safetrax Commuter's share, the rating would be about 4.74★ (directional); the weekly monitor tracks each fix against its baseline.

MIS-01Answer every 1–2★ review within 2 working days, starting with the backlog

Highest priority Owner: App support lead (with Product for routing) Effort: S · ~2 person-days to clear the 90-day backlog, then ~30 min a day

Unanswered reviews: unhappy employees hear nothing back, and every new employee reading the store page sees the complaints left unanswered. The evidence gives the current reply rates for MoveInSync and the closest comparison app.

The change

  • Reply to every unanswered 1–2★ review from the last 90 days, most-upvoted first, using the issue-specific templates in the reply playbook (address the specific problem, give a concrete next step or the fix's release, never ask for a rating change).
  • From then on, reply to every new 1–2★ review within 2 working days and to 3★ reviews weekly. When a fix ships, post a short follow-up reply on the reviews that named it.
  • Route each review to the owner in the playbook (tagging already assigns the issue and owner); service complaints go to the helpdesk queue with the employer named if the review gives it.
  • Give one named person ownership of the Play Console reply queue, with a back-up.

Evidence

  • Reviews answered: 98% in 2024Q1 (last quarter at 90% or more); at most 10% in every quarter since 2024Q3; last reply 18 Aug 2026.

  • Safetrax Commuter, last 365 days: answers 100% of reviews and 100% of 1–2★ reviews (58 of 58; 95% CI 94%–100%).
  • 639 of 9,802 reviews that got a reply (6.5%) were edited after it. Play keeps only the latest stars, so whether replies raise ratings is measured from now on (star-change log).

Expected effect

Google notifies the reviewer of a reply by push and email, and the reviewer can update the rating. The size of the effect is not measurable from past public data; the star-change log measures it from now on.

Success metric

Share of 1–2★ reviews answered (monitor), and star changes after a reply (review_changes log).

Confirm with (internal)

Acceptance criteria

MIS-04Fix the "not downloaded from Play Store" false block

Highest priority Owner: Mobile engineering + Platform (integrity check) Effort: M · 3–5 engineer-days

Employees who installed from Google Play are told every few days that the app "was not downloaded from Play Store" and must reinstall. The pattern (works, then fails days later) points to an integrity check that fails closed on transient errors or stale verdicts, not to sideloading.

The change

  • Day 1 — pull the server-side integrity verdict logs for blocked sessions (verdict fields, exception codes, Play Services version, installer) to confirm the cause.
  • Never block on a transient or remediable error — call isRemediable() and show the Play Integrity GET_INTEGRITY remediation dialog (it can update Play Services or fix network issues); for an UNLICENSED verdict show the GET_LICENSED dialog instead of a block-and-reinstall message.
  • Do not re-run the check while offline; cache the last good verdict for the session.

Evidence

  • 'Not downloaded from Play Store' error: in 3.9% of 1–2★ reviews in the last 90 days (8 of 205; 95% CI 2.0%–7.5%); 0.9% in the 90 days before (rising, p = 0.044).
  • Rating cost rank #19 of 29: −0.015★ on the 365-day written-review average; named in 17 reviews in that period.

MIS-06Sign-in that works — OTP delivery fallback and fewer forced logouts

High priority Owner: Platform / backend (OTP) + Product & design (session rules) Effort: M · 1 sprint

Employees cannot get in — the OTP does not arrive — and are logged out often, so they need a new OTP at the moment the cab arrives. Each forced logout is another chance for OTP delivery to fail.

The change

  • Measure OTP delivery per operator and channel (sent → delivered → entered) and alert when delivery drops.
  • Offer a second channel (email, WhatsApp or voice) automatically after 30 seconds, with a visible resend timer; use the Android SMS Retriever API so the OTP fills itself.
  • Keep sessions alive with refresh tokens; never end a session during a booked trip window; where the employer's policy allows, re-authenticate with the device screen lock instead of a new OTP.
  • List employers whose settings force frequent re-login and share with the account team (OPS-05).

MIS-08Ask happy riders for a rating — in-app review prompt after good trips

High priority Owner: Mobile engineering + Product Effort: S · 2–3 engineer-days After MIS-05

Employees who rate without writing a review count in the store rating but never appear in reviews; the evidence gives their share and average stars. Asking them on Play at a good moment is the cheapest way to add recent positive ratings. The app's own post-trip rating pop-up, by contrast, is itself a complaint. (If the app already calls the In-App Review API, this ticket becomes tuning when it fires.)

The change

  • Call the Google Play In-App Review API after a positive moment — for example the 3rd completed on-time trip in a week with no complaint raised — at most once every few months per user.
  • Never precede it with a question such as "Do you like the app?", never trigger it from a button, and never show it after a late, cancelled or complained-about trip (Google's rules).
  • Do not stack it with the post-trip feedback pop-up; make that pop-up skippable.
  • Ship only after the crash fix (MIS-05) is live, so the prompt reaches users on a stable build.

Evidence

  • 79% of the 114,880 ratings have no text; they average 4.91★, vs 3.61★ for written reviews (approximate: Google rescales its star counts).
  • About 87% of the store's 1★ ratings come with a written review: low ratings are almost all written, while most ratings without text are 5★.
  • Forced trip rating / feedback: in 2.9% of 1–2★ reviews in the last 90 days (6 of 205; 95% CI 1.3%–6.2%); 1.8% in the 90 days before (no significant change, p = 0.465).
  • Rating cost rank #13 of 29: −0.024★ on the 365-day written-review average; named in 25 reviews in that period.

Method, accuracy and limits

  • Data. All 24,317 public Google Play reviews of com.moveinsync.ets (Mar 2014 to 11 Oct 2026, India store, English), the store listing, and 3 comparable employee-commute apps' listings and replies. Reviewer names are never stored.
  • Tagging. 10,311 of 10,311 reviews since Jan 2023 are tagged with up to three of 33 issues built from the employee journey, each with a fixed owner and root-cause type. Short praise is tagged by rule; the rest by nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b on NVIDIA's free NIM tier, with nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b as fallback.
  • Accuracy on 120 held-out, labelled reference reviews: main issue correct 99% (95% CI 95%–100%), tag precision 99%, recall 90%, 0 safety reviews missed. Recall below 100% means issue shares slightly undercount.
  • Statistics. “1–2★ share” = share of 1–2★ reviews in the window naming the issue (95% Wilson interval, n shown). Changes: two-proportion z-test; “emerging” = last 30 days vs the 180 before (at least 3 mentions); releases are Bonferroni-corrected. Rating cost splits each review evenly between the issues it names, so costs add up; it is an upper bound on what a fix can recover.
  • Assumptions. Scenarios keep today's star mix within 1–2★ and 3–5★ and assume the store's 1–2★ share moves with written 1–2★ reviews (about 87% of 1★ ratings carry text). Effort estimates and internal targets are proposals for the owning teams to confirm.
  • Limits. Written reviews are about 21% of ratings and skew negative: they show what goes wrong and how often relative to other problems, not how many trips go wrong. There is no access to crash, OTP, GPS or trip data — each ticket names the internal metric that would confirm it. Employer settings are invisible from outside. Google's rating formula is private.
  • Google Play facts used (replies notify users, reply and in-app review rules, recency weighting, Android vitals thresholds, Play Integrity remediation): sources in docs/SOURCES.md. Full method: docs/METHOD.md.
  • Downloads. backlog.xlsx · backlog_jira.csv (Jira import) · reply_playbook.md.
  • Tools. Python 3.12, pandas, SQLite, google-play-scraper, NVIDIA NIM (Nemotron-3) through the OpenAI-compatible API, Jinja2 with hand-built SVG charts, openpyxl, Typer; pytest, ruff, mypy. Regenerate everything with uv run ratings recommend.
