The question

Why are the employee app's Play Store ratings low, and exactly what should be fixed first? The app is rated 4.64★ from 114,880 ratings, and 6.4% of them are 1★ (Safetrax Commuter: 4.72★, 3.8% 1★). I wanted an answer a product team could act on this week and check a month later, so the brief became: find what drives 1–2★ reviews, rank it by what it costs the rating, and give each fix an owner and a way to measure it.

The data

I downloaded all 24,317 public Google Play reviews of com.moveinsync.ets (Mar 2014 to 11 Oct 2026, India store, English), the store listing with its star counts, and for comparison Safetrax Commuter, WhistleDrive EmployeeApp and SMT Employee: their listings plus 177 reviews and replies from the last 365 days. Reviewer names are never stored.

Sorting reviews into issues

I read a sample of recent complaints and grouped them along the employee journey (Sign-in & onboarding → App stability & speed → Booking & schedules → Pickup time & routing → Live tracking & alerts → Boarding & check-in → Contacting driver → Cab, driver & helpdesk → Safety → Ratings, pop-ups & UI) into 33 issues, each with a fixed owner and root-cause type. 10,311 of the 10,311 reviews since Jan 2023 are tagged with up to three issues: short praise by a rule, the rest by nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b on NVIDIA's free NIM tier, with nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b as fallback.

To know how far to trust the tags, I scored them against 120 reference reviews labelled before any model output existed and never used for tuning (measured 11 Oct 2026): the main issue is correct in 99% (95% CI 95%–100%), tag precision is 99% and recall 90%, and 0 of 2 safety reviews were missed. Recall under 100% means issue shares slightly undercount.

Key decisions, and why

  • Models chosen by measured accuracy, at no cost. I tried the free NVIDIA NIM models and kept the ones that answered reliably; on the held-out set nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b got the main issue right in 99% (95% CI 95%–100%, recall 90%), nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b 98% (95% CI 94%–100%, recall 86%), a vote of both 100% (95% CI 97%–100%, recall 87%). The intervals overlap, so I kept the simplest set-up: nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b as the primary model, nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b only filling gaps when it fails.
  • A live site and a ticket-ready backlog instead of slides. 721 reviews arrived in the last 90 days, so a deck would be out of date within weeks. The site refreshes every 2 hours; the 16 tickets import into Jira or open in Excel, each with an owner, acceptance criteria, a success metric and the internal data that would confirm it.
  • Plan text kept separate from the numbers. The judgement (what to change, who owns it, effort) lives in one file; every share, interval and quote is filled in at build time. The evidence updates on each refresh while the plan stays fixed (baselines recorded 09 Oct 2026), and the page flags any new signal no ticket covers — none today.
  • A significance test before calling any change. Every share carries its n and a 95% Wilson interval; “rising”, “falling” and “emerging” need p < 0.05 on a two-proportion test; releases are Bonferroni-corrected (29 compared over 365 days, 0 flagged now). With about 205 reviews rated 1–2★ per 90 days, the fix tracker only calls a fix “improved” when the whole interval clears its baseline.

Limits, and what internal data would confirm

Written reviews are about 21% of ratings and skew negative, so they show what goes wrong and how often relative to other problems, not how many trips go wrong. I had no internal data. Three sources would confirm or size the top fixes: Android vitals (crash and ANR rates per release and phone model), OTP gateway logs (delivery rate and time per operator) and trip data (pickup delays, GPS ping gaps, boarding check-ins). Employer settings are invisible from outside, and Google's rating formula is private, so rating scenarios are directional.

Tools used

Python 3.12, pandas and SQLite; google-play-scraper for public data; NVIDIA NIM (Nemotron-3) through the OpenAI-compatible API for tagging; Jinja2 with hand-built SVG charts; openpyxl for the Excel backlog; Typer and Rich for the ratings command; pytest, ruff and mypy for checks; GitHub Actions and GitHub Pages to refresh and publish every 2 hours. I used AI assistance for code, taxonomy drafting, reference labelling and analysis review.
